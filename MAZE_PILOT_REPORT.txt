Minimal key-maze representation pilot — 16 November 2026

Decision: the prototype works, but this testbed has a cold-start ceiling.
Do not scale this version or claim a demonstrated epistemic blind spot.

The game
Three rooms: start A, key room B, exit C. A--B is open; A--C needs the
reusable key collected automatically on entering B. The valid shortest
route is A -> B -> A -> C. No traps, hidden physics, or extra mechanics.
Diagram: examples/maze-abstraction.png.

The algorithm
Ordered search over small passage graphs and key/door placements found
the example after two candidates. Room-only BFS incorrectly reports no
route because returning to A is discarded as a visited node. The checked
certificate contains two reachable states: A without a key and A with a
key. Moving to C is legal in only the latter. Adding the inventory bit
removes the collision and restores the shortest route.

What was checked mechanically
All four reachable states were enumerated. The certificate checker replays
both histories and checks the claimed difference in legal moves. The same
BFS is used before and after repair. Four prospectively fixed transfer
cases cover renamed rooms, an extended branch, a bypass cycle, and an
ordinary maze with no key. The cycle control can be solved using a flawed
representation, confirming that a successful route alone does not certify
representation sufficiency. Sufficiency is checked separately through
goal labels and action-indexed successor classes on reachable states.

Small live experiment
The frozen design allowed at most six requests and 0.5 additional credits:
two unassisted encoders first, followed by four paired repair calls only
if the cold panel did not already pass. Both models passed, so the four
generic-versus-certificate feedback calls were skipped as specified.

The original shared 190-credit cap was not changed or reset.

Interpretation
This validates a small, understandable counterexample generator and
mechanical representation checker. It does not demonstrate an advantage
over generic feedback, self-diagnosis, or discovery of a new concept.
The prompt explicitly supplies room and has_key as observations, so the
live task tests representation selection rather than predicate invention.
Known abstraction-refinement ideas are not made novel by this feasibility
check. The larger research hypothesis remains untested by a discriminating
case. A future design should require discovering the missing state from
events, without adding complicated game mechanics; that is not run here.
