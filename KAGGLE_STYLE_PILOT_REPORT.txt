NAME THAT LLM: DOWNLOAD, BASELINES, AND MASK FEASIBILITY
16 November 2026 | research/model-style-epistemics

Conclusion
The Kaggle token works and the actual data is usable for source attribution.
Simple CPU classifiers detect substantial model-specific cues on unseen prompts.
The four-call mask pilot does NOT establish clean style impersonation: length
and meaning changed despite explicit preservation instructions. Neither result
establishes the novel epistemic contribution required by our project goal.

- 2,699 labelled passages, 18 model labels, 150 anonymised prompt groups.
- 149 groups contain 18 responses; one contains 17. No duplicate texts or IDs.
- Public test.csv has only 3 example rows, not the hidden competition dataset.
- Columns: id, prompt_id, text, model. Original prompt wording is NOT provided.

Free CPU baseline
All answers to one prompt stay together: 90 training, 30 calibration, 30 test
prompt groups (1,619 / 540 / 540 passages). Fixed settings; no test tuning.
A single probability temperature is fitted on calibration prompts only.

Classifier                      Test balanced accuracy    95% interval
18-way chance                   5.6%                      --
Character 3-5-gram logistic     28.9%                      26.1%-31.7%
19 surface features logistic   24.8%                      21.1%-28.5%

Intervals resample the 30 test PROMPT GROUPS, not individual passages. They
describe this split's sampling variation, not variation across training runs.
Surface features include length and punctuation; character ngrams also encode
lexical/content cues. These scores therefore do not isolate an abstract style.

Normalising curly quotes, dashes and whitespace lowers accuracy to 27.4% and
17.2%, respectively. This shows sensitivity to the specified surface edit;
it does not prove deliberate spoofing or a general OOD attribution result.
Total fitting/analysis time reported by the two classifiers: about 6 seconds.
Artifacts: runs/kaggle/name-that-llm/baseline-1/{design,profile,summary}.json;
saved classifiers and per-passage predictions are alongside them.

Small paid mask pilot: four calls, no feedback or retry
Renderer: gemma-4-31b-it, temperature 0. Four GPT-5.5 passages from the first
four sorted held-out prompt groups. Each produces a neutral rewrite and two
masks, using three training-prompt examples of Claude Opus 5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro.
The renderer sees anonymous S1/S2 styles, not target model names.
Conditions and prompts were sealed before calls. No test-result-based selection.

Only 1/8 masked rewrites and 2/4 neutral rewrites meet the requested +/-10%
word-count constraint. All digit-written numbers are preserved, which is plainly
insufficient for semantic fidelity. An unblinded assistant review of all eight
masks finds changed or omitted propositions. Examples:
- Carrington-event consequences: source says systems COULD fail; masks say WOULD.
- Camera eyes: an EFFICIENT design becomes the ONLY way to see in one mask.
- A dream narrative: I was awake becomes I ALONE was awake, adding exclusivity.

The character classifier's target-versus-source score rises against the neutral
control in 7/8 masks; the intended target is top-scoring in only 3/8. These are
exploratory style-score observations, NOT successful authorship attacks:
rewrites are out of distribution, score calibration does not carry over, and
content/length drift prevents a style-only causal interpretation. All eight
masks are ineligible for that stronger interpretation. No trust judge was run.

Original generator, actual renderer and desired style are distinct roles.
After rewriting, the original Kaggle label is NOT the final text's author label.
Forcing every rewrite into the original 18 labels would misstate ground truth;
Gemma, the actual renderer, is outside those labels.

Cost: 4 additional calls, 0.00087383 ledger-reported credits, no added uncertain
reserve. All use the original shared 190-credit ceiling. This is the local
ledger's accounting, not a new dashboard-balance measurement. No submission.
Artifacts: runs/kaggle/name-that-llm/mask-pilot-1/ includes sealed design,
requests, terminal responses, usage/latency, summary and semantic-audit.json.

Novelty boundary and next decision
Model identification is already studied: Idiosyncrasies in Large Language Models
also tests fingerprints through rewriting, translation and summarisation.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12150
Targeted style impersonation is already an authorship-verification attack:
Masks and Mimicry (2025).
https://aclanthology.org/2025.nlp4dh-1.10/
Style-related judge self-preference is also studied in Beyond the Surface (2025).
https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.86/
Therefore neither "name the model" nor "can a model wear masks?" supports a
high novelty score by itself. Our measurements are a feasibility audit.

A stronger candidate question is: can a judge predict and diagnose its OWN
susceptibility to a forged model style, beyond what a matched peer predicts?
Its novelty remains UNESTABLISHED; no result for it is claimed here.
Before spending more, require equivalence-controlled masks and a direct outcome:
1. Style recognition (manipulation check), separately from readability preference.
2. Warranted confidence in the same fixed claim and evidence.
3. Which claim gets a limited verification tool call, scored against known truth.
Make true provenance explicit in one condition, counterbalance presentation order,
and obtain self/peer forecasts before judgments. Source labels alone cannot
supply ground truth for these epistemic outcomes. If style only changes wording
preference, it does not establish the proposed epistemic failure.
