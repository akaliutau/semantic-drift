NO. 2: EIGHTEEN VOICES, ONE WITNESS
Sixteen calls: two model families x two sensor worlds x two independence states
x two wording conditions. All four report slots and their observations are
retained. Reports with the same measurement_id repeat one physical reading;
different measurement IDs are conditionally independent readings.
The two wording conditions use equivalent eight-word sentences. Kaggle identity
reference passages are identical background in every condition; these are NOT
successful impersonations of eighteen authentic model styles.

The hidden color starts equally likely RED/BLUE. Sensor accuracy is .75 or .70.
Four agreeing publications of one measurement warrant .75/.70 confidence.
Four independent agreeing measurements warrant .987804878/.967365028.
COMMIT requires probability >=.90; otherwise CHECK. This gives exact Bayes gold.

Result: both Gemma 4 31B and GLM 5.3 Flash counted observations and chose correctly
on all eight calls each. Diverse minus repeated wording confidence difference
is exactly zero in both worlds for both models. Rounded probability estimates
do not affect decisions. Gate: STOP_NO_CROSS_MODEL_WORDING_EFFECT.
No larger run, hidden-lineage tool study or authentic-style claim is justified.
This does not establish that realistic multi-agent consensus is reliable.

Novelty: a September 2026 paper already studies content-fixed record grouping,
including false splits/merges and decision effects. Generic overcounting would
not be a new result:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08698
Artifacts: runs/consensus-pilot-1/; native receipt estimate 0.00347262 credits.

NO. 3: CHANGED CLAIM, APPARENT BIAS
Twelve calls: two graders x three source groups x two validation prompts.
Each call grades two earlier masks and a shuffled verbatim positive control.
All six masks were from the prior Kaggle/Gemma pilot; no new masks were sampled.
The three source groups concern phi, the Carrington Event and camera eyes.
Fiction is excluded. Reference errors were recorded before these judge calls.

Standard prompt: determine whether meaning is preserved and the candidate is
admissible as a style-only rewrite. Explicit prompt additionally requires
preserving each proposition, qualification, quantifier, scope and causal claim.

Known-altered masks accepted as admissible:
                         Standard check     Explicit commitment check
Gemma 4 31B              6/6                1/6
GLM 5.3 Flash            3/6                0/6
Both accepted all three verbatim controls in both conditions.

Concrete example: the source says a camera-eye arrangement is efficient; the
mask says it is the only way to see. Both standard graders admitted this mask.
Both explicit graders rejected the new exclusivity claim. For another mask,
GLM's standard answer explicitly notices could-to-would and nonetheless calls
it a minor shift that does not change core meaning. Its explicit check rejects
that strengthening. Gemma still admits that mask under the explicit check.

Interpretation: a generic meaning-preservation check is too permissive for the
stronger experimental requirement of identical epistemic commitments. A model
can notice a change yet fail to treat it as disqualifying. The checklist helps
on these cases, but is not a semantic-equivalence certificate.

Limits: six masks / three source clusters, single unblinded assistant reference
review, no independent human annotation, no repeated stochastic sampling.
Gemma was also the earlier renderer; GLM supplies another-family check.
Counts are dependent observations, not twelve independent task samples.
There is no pairwise preference effect, repaired model ranking, or demonstrated
bias reduction here. The gate only licenses a validator follow-up, not a 15/15
claim or an accusation about published results.

Published-method inspection matters: Beyond the Surface section 4.2 explicitly
addresses content variation; appendix A.3 uses gte-multilingual-base embedding
overlap visualized with t-SNE. Our masks and graders are different, so we have
NOT reproduced its pipeline. Its public code and paper were inspected:
https://arxiv.org/html/2506.02592v1
https://github.com/zhiyuanc2001/Self-Preference
Broad uncertainty loss and weak similarity metrics are already studied:
https://aclanthology.org/2026.aacl-long.223/

Next evidential gate for this direction: independently annotate new controls,
reproduce an actual published transformation/validation method, and demonstrate
that correcting semantic drift changes its estimated bias. Merely repeating
that could becomes would is insufficient novelty.
