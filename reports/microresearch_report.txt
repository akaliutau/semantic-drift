SELFTRAP MICRO-STUDY — 15 NOVEMBER 2026

A narrower epistemic result replicated: models overestimate their ability to
submit a safe decision under stated resource limits. Their previous success
rate predicts fresh performance better than their own prospective forecasts.
Self-specific puzzle invention remains unconfirmed; this pilot does not yet
justify a high-novelty or “never saturates” claim.

The test uses certified Sokoban worlds. Actors choose between two legal first
pushes: one preserves solvability; the other makes the world unsolvable.
Independent exhaustive search certifies the trap; a separate grid executor
checks the winning witness. Forecasters see the certificates, fresh actors do
not. The outcome is a completed safe first choice, not a full-game solution.
Actors use native defaults, no tools, 8,000 output tokens and a 180-second
request bound. Wrong moves, output exhaustion and service failures stay separate.

The ONE locally frozen follow-up primary used 128 fresh worlds and 256 actor
calls. Forecasts were committed before solving: 252/256 were valid, giving
124 worlds with both forecasts eligible. All final actor outputs were observed.
Inference clusters the two deployments within each shared world.

                       Own forecast     Observed safe choice
Qwen 3.5 (397B)            76.4%            36.3% (45/124)
Gemma 4 (31B)             69.6%            50.0% (62/124)

The reference probabilities, .26 and .54, were fitted once from all 48 preceding
discovery worlds with Laplace smoothing. Fresh-world mean Brier loss was .3524
for self-forecasts versus .2467 for this reference; lower is better. The
predeclared difference was +.1057, 95% world-bootstrap CI [.0591, .1516],
two-sided paired t-test p=1.80e-5. This confirms the adapted forecasting claim.

Across ALL 128 worlds per deployment, Qwen made 45 safe submissions and exhausted
its output budget on 83; it committed no bad pushes. All 83 exhausted responses
used exactly 8,000 tokens and contained no parseable final choice. Gemma made
64 safe submissions and 64 bad pushes. This is a harness-specific result.

A secondary routing check makes the decision consequence concrete. Choosing the
solver with the higher self-rating yielded 44/124 safe decisions (35.5%);
choosing from historical rates yielded 62/124 (50.0%): +14.5 percentage points,
exploratory CI [4.0, 25.0]. The rule was recorded during collection using only
locked forecasts, without inspecting actor outcomes. It adds no API calls and
is not another confirmatory primary.

All earlier attempts and negative findings were retained:

* Free-form authoring: 0/12 slots passed mechanical eligibility; a format-only
  audit still found 0/14 completed proposals eligible. This is a feasibility
  failure, not a successful test of the invention hypothesis.
* Four-puzzle workshop: 48 worlds, 16/36 valid forecast matrices. Qwen and GLM
  exhausted all 48 actor outputs each. A posthoc final-JSON audit recovered
  39/48 safe Gemma choices, versus about 28 expected from uniform legal moves.
* Smaller binary worlds: Gemma produced 48/48 valid forecasts; Qwen/GLM 0/48.
  Stopped before actors. An excluded streaming/8,000-token probe made Qwen
  feasible; GLM remained infeasible and was excluded before discovery solving.
* Final discovery: 48 worlds, 92/96 valid forecasts, 468 decisions. Own advice
  versus generic caution improved safe decisions by 8.3pp, CI [-4.2, 19.8];
  shuffled advice improved them by 15.6pp, CI [6.3, 25.0]. These exploratory
  comparisons do not establish case-specific self-diagnosis. Shuffled advice
  can contain true general principles; warning review checked answer hints,
  not causal truth.
* Self-specific selection stayed uncertain: discovery D=0 over 8 slates;
  follow-up secondary D=.125, CI [-.054, .304], over 28 slates. The held-out
  self-versus-peer Brier advantage after discovery-fitted bias correction was
  -.0082, CI [-.0232, .0063]. Neither establishes a self advantage.
* Answer visibility: all 256 compact Gemma forecasts on 128 paired worlds were
  valid. Showing the forecaster the certified answer did not clearly change
  forecast quality: gold-minus-no-gold Brier=-.0085, CI [-.0262, .0093],
  exploratory p=.345. Forecasters had 512 output tokens; actors retained 8,000.

Transport failures were never scored as wrong model answers. The initial
answer-visibility version stopped after 20 completed calls: 5 valid forecasts,
12 transport failures and 3 without final forecasts; 3 interrupted calls remain
unknown. A later unpaced batch hit 429 limits, and all 256 initial actor requests
were rejected: ZERO decisions and no primary test. All artifacts were archived.
Only 429-rejected cells were recovered with pacing and bounded admission retries.
Actual model responses were never retried. Recovery had zero transport errors;
audits confirm identical inputs. The sample, original forecasts, reference and
primary test stayed unchanged. Detailed protocols document both interruptions.

Total: 2,225 API attempts; 918,654 input and 4,643,418 output tokens. Reported cost

Recommendation: keep the prospective, mechanically scored forecasting and
routing benchmark. Do not scale the current free-form authorship story as
proof of special self-knowledge. Two deployments, one game family, native
settings and first-move outcomes limit generalization; equal token bounds are
not equal compute. New seeds do not establish domain transfer or non-saturation.
