RELATED-WORK CHECK DURING THE MICRO-STUDY, 15 NOVEMBER 2026

The broad claims are already covered. A successful pilot must not be sold as
the first demonstration that self-reports can be generic or overconfident.

Strangers to Themselves (Blandfort & Pawar, September2026) directly compares
self-specific behavioral predictions with generic-agent and other-model
controls. Its reported self advantage is weak, and favorable self framing is
an important bias. This is especially close to our original crossed forecast
question; our null constructive D cannot be presented as overturning it.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09899

Generalized Correctness Models (Xiao et al., ICML2026) finds little privileged
self-correctness advantage and learns prediction from historical correctness,
including in-context history and calibration. Our constant-rate holdout is a
simple operational baseline in the same broad line, not a new calibration idea.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24988

CRATER (Jiang et al., November4,2026) explicitly separates correct, incorrect
and unfinished reasoning under token budgets, fitting distributions for
configuration/budget routing and repeated sampling. Our three observed outcome
types are necessary measurement practice, not an invented taxonomy.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05860

Looking Inward (Binder et al., ICLR2025) studies trained self-prediction and
reports advantages on some simple tasks but difficulties with more complex or
out-of-distribution tasks. Our prompted API study has no self-prediction training
and cannot settle questions about privileged internal access.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787

MathDuels already documents incorrect answers to self-generated valid problems.
Our failed free-form Sokoban round adds a feasibility observation; workshop
selection alone does not reproduce the claim of novel free-form authorship.
https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21916v3#A3.SS3

The secondary answer-visibility ablation targets a narrower information
boundary: does a correct private solution change forecasts of an actor that
never sees it? Related areas include biased reference-conditioned judges and
knowledge leakage in role-play. We have not established that this ablation is
globally novel; a positive result would be a specific empirical lead.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03419
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17327
