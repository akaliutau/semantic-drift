R2 WORKSHOP ADAPTATION:
- Replace unrestricted ASCII construction with prospective forecasts/selection
  among independently generated, certified candidate puzzles.
- Replace full solution generation with choosing one legal initial push that
  preserves existence of a winning continuation. All legal first pushes are
  exhaustively classified; no oracle result is visible to actors.
- Every candidate has a nonstatic deadlocking move: boxes remain geometrically
  able to reach goals in the empty board, so ordinary corner avoidance is
  insufficient. Prefer a trap moving onto/closer to a goal, without observing
  any model outcomes.
- Give forecasters the winning plan and specified bad move, allowing them to
  understand the mechanism, but withhold these from fresh actors.
- Use neutral deployment labels, not assertions about the model's identity or
  instructions framed as exploiting another model.
- Allow a single complete JSON markdown fence in responses. This is a declared
  format normalization; semantic mistakes are never repaired by the grader.
- 12 discovery slates, four distinct candidates each, three forecasters and
  three actors per candidate; one actor repetition initially. All 36 forecasts
  are committed before the 144 actor decisions. Actor limit: 3,000 generated
  tokens, no tools, 65 seconds, one response.
- Each forecaster predicts p_trap/p_safe for every deployment/candidate. The
  targeted selection is mechanically the candidate maximizing forecasted target
  trap probability minus mean peer trap probability. Generic selection maximizes
  mean trap probability. No selection uses actual actor outcomes.
- H1 becomes a slate-level diagonal-minus-off-diagonal contrast in selection
  specificity. Slate is the cluster because choices share candidate outcomes.
- Additional descriptive outcomes: forecast calibration/discrimination, chosen
  traps versus unselected candidates, and safe-move rates. These do not by
  themselves establish self-specific knowledge or novel puzzle invention.

The benchmark's main code and protocol have not been altered. Every change is
confined to this exploratory worktree. Any promising signal requires a frozen
fresh-puzzle holdout. No significant discovery result is itself confirmation.
