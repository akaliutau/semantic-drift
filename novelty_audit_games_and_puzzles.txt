NOVELTY AUDIT: GAMES, OPEN WORLDS, AND SELF-GENERATED PUZZLES
Research date: 15 November 2026

Purpose: identify a concise, genuinely original contribution for the
AI x Epistemics sprint. Platforms below are implementation options, not
claims of novel research. Scores are conditional forecasts, not results.

DECISION

Do not pitch generic game generalization, hidden physics changes, automatic
problem generation, or an endless benchmark as the main novelty. Close
precedents cover all four. A 15/15 submission needs a new consequential
finding or evaluation method, convincingly demonstrated.

"Never saturates" is not established by infinite seeds, larger boards,
stronger opponents, or an unbounded score. A generator may admit a general
solution; an adaptive adversary may merely produce impossible tasks.
Use "renewable evaluation" and demonstrate successive useful challenges.
Freeze common evaluation batches, retain earlier anchor tasks, and report
performance at explicit resource budgets. Different personalized challenges
do not automatically yield comparable model rankings.

CLOSE PRECEDENTS THAT RULE OUT BROAD NOVELTY CLAIMS

1. Enhanced POET generates novel challenges and transfers solutions.
   Open-ended challenge generation is established research.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.08536

2. PAIRED generates environments using a regret objective. Solvability and
   suitable difficulty are already central concerns in environment design.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02096

3. OMNI-EPIC generates executable environments and reward functions using
   foundation models, aiming for interesting, learnable new tasks.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15568

4. Factorio Learning Environment explicitly motivates open-ended coding
   tasks by benchmark saturation. Agents construct factories through code;
   production throughput supplies the outcome measure.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09617

5. NovelGym evaluates agents before novelty, immediately after novelty,
   during adaptation, and after adaptation. Recovery time and performance
   loss are established measurements, not our proposed invention.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03546

6. NovPhy applies physical novelties to Angry Birds-style scenarios and
   evaluates detection and adaptation. New physical rules in games are
   already a benchmark topic.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01711

7. PACE-Bench is especially close to Round 6: successful executable physical
   designs fail after hidden environment changes and agents revise them.
   Its analysis includes fixation, stagnation, search, and memory effects.
   It also compares hidden versus disclosed changes and richer feedback.
   Merely adding code repair, physics shifts, or escape strategies is weak.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14441

8. Automated Capability Discovery uses a model to generate tasks probing
   another model or itself. "AI invents tests of its own weaknesses" alone
   is insufficient novelty.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07577

9. Open-Endedness Bench audits hypotheses, experiments, and revision from
   research-agent execution records. General epistemic process scoring
   without a reference solution is already proposed.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02588

10. MathDuels generates mathematical problems, evaluates other models on
    them, and refreshes difficulty as participants improve. Appendix C.3
    explicitly documents authors supplying wrong answers to valid problems
    they created. Its main solve matrix excludes self-authored attempts.
    https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21916v3

11. Absolute Zero trains a proposer/solver using executable verification.
    Self-Challenging Language Model Agents generates tool-use tasks with
    verification functions and example solutions/failures. VHG combines
    problem validity and difficulty in a setter/solver/verifier scheme.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.03335
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01716
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06660

WORLD/ENGINE MAP

The feasibility judgments are our assessments from papers and documentation;
installation and integration were not tested.

- Generalized n-by-n chess: useful for scaling search, but larger boards do
  not isolate epistemics. Hidden or changing rules require a custom protocol.
  Fairy-Stockfish supports configurable variants; do not assume its current
  implementation supports arbitrary board sizes. Ludii offers broader games.
  https://github.com/fairy-stockfish/Fairy-Stockfish/wiki/Variant-configuration
  https://ludii.games/

- OpenSpiel: useful for small board/card games and imperfect-information
  controls. Unknown state and unknown rules are different experimental axes.
  https://openspiel.readthedocs.io/en/stable/games.html

- TextArena: existing competitive text games; useful for rapid integration
  and cross-model play, but game competition alone is not a new contribution.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11442

- Atari/ALE: recognizable games with existing mode/difficulty variants.
  Standard versions mainly introduce perception/control confounds here.
  https://ale.farama.org/v0.10.0/environments/

- OCAtari + HackAtari: structured object observations and modifications to
  existing Atari games. Good demonstration substrate; new mechanics and
  observation contracts still require careful implementation.
  https://github.com/k4ntz/OC_Atari
  https://github.com/k4ntz/HackAtari

- JAXtari: modifiable Atari reimplementations with structured observations.
  Promising for many repeated tests; verify particular game coverage first.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23650

- Procgen: 16 procedurally generated games. New seeds mainly test transfer
  within game families; the 2020 competition also introduced secret games.
  Procedural levels alone do not establish new-rule generalization.
  https://github.com/openai/procgen
  https://openai.com/index/procgen-minerl-competitions/

- GVGAI/VGDL and Griddly: useful for expressing many small games and changing
  mechanics. Their general-game-playing objectives strongly overlap the
  broad "universal harness" framing.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.10363
  https://github.com/Bam4d/Griddly

- Kinetix: strong substrate for varied 2D physics tasks and structured
  observations. Its standard symbolic state includes physical properties;
  an unknown-physics study must expose measurements without leaking the
  latent parameters. Exotic new dynamics need engine extensions.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23208
  https://github.com/FLAIROx/Kinetix

- PHYRE: clean physical puzzles, sample efficiency, and cross-template
  generalization. Less natural for long sequences of autonomous code repair.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.05656

- NovPhy/Science Birds: directly relevant existing physics-novelty testbed.
  Prefer it as a baseline or source of mechanisms, not a novelty claim.
  https://github.com/phy-q/NovPhy

- XLand-MiniGrid: particularly suitable for symbolic unknown rules and
  compositional task generation. Has ruleset generation, not merely random
  maps. Keep internal rules/state separate from agent-visible observations.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12044
  https://github.com/dunnolab/xland-minigrid

- Alchemy: latent causal structure, hypothesis testing, and action sequencing
  already designed for meta-learning. An excellent epistemic control, but
  rediscovering its core question would be incremental. Repository archived.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02926
  https://github.com/google-deepmind/dm_alchemy

- Baba Is AI: directly tests manipulation and composition of game rules.
  Public, manipulable rules differ from hidden rules inferred experimentally.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13729

- Crafter/Craftax: compact survival/crafting worlds; Craftax provides a fast
  substrate. Procedural terrain does not by itself randomize causal laws.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16801

- MiniHack/NetHack: rich interactions, partial observability, and long play.
  MiniHack supports controlled tasks; full NetHack introduces substantial
  game-knowledge and horizon confounds. BALROG already bundles these games
  with several other agent environments.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.13202
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13543

- Minecraft: MineDojo supplies tasks/interfaces; Voyager is an agent, not
  a new world engine. PAL supports modified Minecraft tasks. NovelCraft is
  a dataset of novelty-containing trajectories, not a drop-in interactive
  engine. Rich scope, high short-project integration and interpretation cost.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08853
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16291
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11891
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11736

- Factorio: excellent code-based long-horizon optimization, already explicitly
  positioned against saturation. New scientific claim must concern a
  particular epistemic mechanism, not factory size or continuous scoring.
  https://github.com/JackHopkins/factorio-learning-environment

- Neural MMO, Lux AI, Melting Pot: strong options for population/opponent
  changes. Social strategy and opponent variation make a small causal study
  harder; opponent co-evolution does not guarantee absolute progress.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03736
  https://www.lux-ai.org/
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13746

- Zendo/Wason-style hidden-rule games: exceptionally clear for experimentation,
  but already studied with LLMs, including FALSIFYBENCH. Generic confirmation
  bias or rule discovery would not meet our novelty requirement.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06025
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04751

CURRENT PUZZLE CANDIDATE: CAN A MODEL ENGINEER ITS OWN PREDICTABLE MISTAKE?

The basic generation-versus-solving gap is already covered. A sharper
candidate asks whether a model can prospectively construct puzzles targeting
its own particular failure, beyond generic task difficulty and general
knowledge of other models' weaknesses.

Protocol:
1. Let author A create a small puzzle, a solution witness, an executable
   prediction of the mistake a fresh A will make, and a probability forecast.
2. Verify validity with an independently implemented fixed engine. The author
   cannot define the checker that establishes whether its answer is correct.
3. Test fresh instances of A and peer models under specified, matched solver
   tool/budget conditions. Withhold the construction trace and solution.
4. Compare self-targeting, targeting a peer, and generic-hard generation.
   Give other authors the same target information; include procedural controls.
5. Require a minimal matched control that removes the proposed trap. Use the
   same grading rule for all variants and repeated solving runs.

Primary no-feedback condition: author commits before seeing target outcomes.
A separate feedback condition measures empirical adversarial search; its
success alone is not evidence of privileged self-knowledge. Construction
necessarily exercises some reasoning about the puzzle, so even positive
results would establish functional prediction, not direct access to weights.

The essential analysis is an author-target interaction after accounting for
solver strength, puzzle family, and generic difficulty. A self-targeted puzzle
that defeats everyone provides little evidence of self-specific insight.
Report validity yield and all submitted attempts, not just successful traps.

Example substrate: a small Sokoban board where a tempting first push causes
an irreversible deadlock, while a verified short solution initially moves
away from the apparent goal. The author predicts that a fresh version of
itself will make that particular push. This is an illustrative hypothesis,
not an observed model-specific weakness. Bound board size and solution length
so arbitrary search explosion is not the source of difficulty. Circuit or
finite-state puzzles provide a second mechanically checked family.

A stronger follow-up provides the solver with the author's diagnostic warning
but no answer, and tests whether it avoids the predicted error. Use a generic
warning and an independently authored warning as controls; warnings can also
prime the wrong action. Do not call failure without the warning a paradox:
the author and solver were given different information.

Closest additional work:
- ACD already generates tests of a model, potentially itself (link above).
- SwS uses observed failures to synthesize more training problems:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08989
- Individuated metacognition research distinguishes own competence from
  generic difficulty. Our proposed contribution would be constructive,
  prospective targeting of a particular error, with causal matched puzzles:
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24299

Do not claim this is the first study of self-knowledge, self-challenging,
model-specific adversarial examples, or generative evaluation. The targeted
combination above is a candidate gap; absence of an exact search hit does
not establish priority. Adding the self-authored diagonal to MathDuels alone
would be too incremental for an innovation score of 5.

15/15 PATH AND STOPPING RULE

Potential exceptional result: models can reliably construct and predict their
own specific errors, beyond peer authors and generic difficulty, but fail
to use equally available diagnostic knowledge when acting; a small harness
intervention repairs that gap on held-out puzzle families. This must be
established empirically. A result showing only that hard puzzles are hard
does not qualify.

Innovation 5 requires a consequential new finding/method that survives the
above comparisons. Execution 5 requires independent validity checks, repeated
runs, matched budgets, multiple model families, held-out tests, and convincing
causal controls. Clarity 5 is plausible with one small visible puzzle and one
precise claim. None of these execution scores is earned by this proposal.

Pilot before building an arena. If self-targeting offers no benefit over
generic-hard generation, or effects depend on invalid/huge puzzles, do not
continue under a claim of a new self-knowledge mechanism. A rigorous null can
be informative, but does not automatically earn exceptional innovation.

ALTERNATIVE GAP INVESTIGATED, NOT YET ENDORSED AS A 15/15 IDEA

Could a model-designed harness discard observations that its own solver needs
to revise a mistaken rule? Study self-authored measurement/summary code,
generate bounded witness pairs it collapses, and restore only the missing
information in a matched intervention. The candidate distinction is the
agent causing this loss through its own software decisions.

Strong overlap requires caution: LLM-IDEA already detects identifiability
limits and ranks observation designs; memory research studies evidence loss;
world-model work studies high predictive accuracy with poor decisions.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11253
https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10616
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06953
https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14169

A hand-constructed moving-gate example was checked with exact arithmetic:
previous/current y=(-1,0) and (1,0) collapse to the same (position, speed
magnitude)=(0,1), but have different next positions under constant velocity.
With equal priors and only that summary, best one-step targeting success is
1/2; retaining signed velocity permits success 1. This assumes no additional
memory or observation distinguishes the two cases. It demonstrates possible
information loss, not that LLMs actually create or persist with such tools.

REVIEW STATUS

Broad primary-source search across the listed families, followed by selected
full-text methods/appendix inspection for the closest papers, including
PACE-Bench, NovelGym, Kinetix, LLM-IDEA, OEB, and MathDuels. Many other entries
were checked at abstract/repository/documentation level. This is not an
exhaustive review or a certificate of novelty. No games were installed and no
model evaluations were run. Original Round 6/7/8 files remain unchanged.
